The Story

Sometimes it feels like January was invented by a maniacal Batman villain.

Here's why we decided to do something about it — and why Lancaster, Pennsylvania, of all places, is where a new winter holiday season took root.

We end each year with a marathon of celebrations and begin the next one with a monthlong emotional hangover. It happens every year, like clockwork, all across the country. We light our lights, host our parties, sip our cocoa — and then, almost overnight, we box up the cheer and brace for the chill.

It doesn't have to be this way.

In a 2023 YouGov poll, January ranked among Americans' least favorite months of the year — surpassed in malaise only by February. It's easy to assume that's just the weather talking. Pipes freeze. Germs spread. The days are short and getting shorter. But December is darker than January and nearly as cold, and in that same poll, December came out as America's most beloved month.

The difference isn't the weather — it's what we do with it.

December gets dark, so we wrap our world in shimmering lights. We're cooped up, so we sing songs about gathering close to the people we love. That kind of collective joy isn't handed to us by the calendar. It's something we create. It's a choice — and it's a choice other cultures have already made for the rest of the winter, too. In Sweden, where the sun can set before 3 p.m. in January, people lean into candles, soup, tea, and long cold walks followed by a warm seat by the fire, rather than surrendering to the dark. The Danish word for that kind of restful coziness is hygge.

Among a small group of friends in Lancaster County, this new holiday season has already existed for more than a decade in one form or another. We even gave it a name — a pseudo-Swedish word that, according to us, means "cozy days": Cozendagen.

How it grew
~10 years ago

A family tradition, privately kept

Cliff Lewis and his family start marking the deep-winter stretch on their own terms — soup, candlelight, and deliberately cozy nights to counter the January slump. No public events, no name yet in wide use — just a household ritual repeated on purpose, year after year.

2025

The idea goes public

Cliff writes and publishes an editorial making the case for Cozendagen as a real, shareable seasonal tradition — and starts building the @cozendagen Instagram account, sharing the concept, the vocabulary, and the vibe with Lancaster and beyond.

December 2025

The first public Cozendagen Winter Festival is announced

West Art, an anchor of Lancaster's local culture, agrees to transform its Sanctuary into the Cozendagen Ski Lodge for one night — hot soup, hot drinks, hot dessert, live music, and cozy lighting, with local partners like Elsewhere Bread and The Twisted Spoon on board.

January 31, 2026

The inaugural event sells out — and doubles its space

RSVPs outpace expectations so quickly that West Art doubles the footprint of the event. More than 100 guests turn up in fuzzy sweaters, buttons that read "Ask me about Cozendagen," and genuine curiosity about a holiday most of them had never heard of a month earlier.

Spring 2026 →

"Closendagen," and a look ahead

The season wraps with real gratitude and real momentum: a picture-house movie night, a proper clothing drive, local press coverage, and a founder already dreaming up what's next — for winter 2027, and for the cities beyond Lancaster.

There's no better place to build a new tradition than Lancaster County. This is a place that commits to the bit — from the ExtraGive's annual celebration of shared generosity, to First Friday, to Fasnacht Day. Lancaster is already the breadbasket of Pennsylvania, the unofficial capital of Pennsylvania Dutch Country, and a neighborly hub of refugee resettlement. Maybe one day we'll add another line to that list: the birthplace of Cozendagen.

By enshrining these timeless cold-weather comforts as proper Cozendagen traditions — on purpose, and on the regular — we can make deep winter not just survivable, but genuinely delightful. That's the whole idea. Nothing more complicated than that.
