Host a Cozendagen Event

Every city has a West Art. We just need to find it.

West Art didn't just host our first festival — it set the model. We're looking for its equivalent in the next wave of Cozendagen cities: a venue with real local culture, a younger crowd, and the will to own a piece of the night.

Apply to host
What we look for

Two things matter more than anything else. If your venue has both, you're already most of the way there.

1

You can own at least one part of the night

Food, drinks, or dessert — a Cozendagen host doesn't need to do everything, but should be able to fully own one component the way West Art owns drinks and its Sanctuary space, or the way The Twisted Spoon owns soup. We'll help you find partners for the rest.

2

You already have a scene

Cozendagen works because it plugs into a community that already gathers — a younger, active, locally-minded crowd who'll show up for something new because they already show up for you. We're not looking for empty banquet halls; we're looking for rooms that are already a little alive.

What hosting looks like
Before

We build the season together

A shared framework — theme, decor direction, drink/food guidance, and the Cozendagen name and materials — adapted to fit your space and your city.

The Night

One night, fully transformed

Your venue becomes a Cozendagen Ski Lodge for an evening: hot soup, hot drinks, hot dessert, comfy seating, cozy lighting, and a clothing drive at the door.

After

You become the local anchor

Like West Art in Lancaster, your venue becomes the flagship for Cozendagen in your city — first in line for next winter, and part of the story we tell.

Where we're headed

Strong candidates for 2027

Phoenixville and Bethlehem, PA — smaller cities with the walkable, community-anchored culture Cozendagen thrives in.

Under consideration

Harrisburg, Reading, Ambler, State College, and Erie — we're actively scouting each city's West Art.

Beachheads

Philadelphia and Pittsburgh are planned as intentional, smaller-scale beachhead events rather than full activations — the first flag in the ground.
