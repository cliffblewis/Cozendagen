Text on background — measured

The finding that shapes the system: sunset has no body-text pairing at all — its best pair is toffee at 4.37:1. Valentine's best is snow at 3.84:1, large text only. So sunset and valentine are accent grounds, and copy on them goes inside a snow panel. You'll see that in the bands below.

Dusk number = clears 4.5:1 (body). Sunset + L = 3:1, large text only. Faded + ✕ = below 3:1.

02 — Typography

03 — Bands

A page is a stack of bands

Two ground types, alternated. Light: snow background with dusk, valentine, or sunset as the text colour. Colour: dusk, valentine, flame, or sunset background with snow text. No bread bands, no toffee bands. All band content is centered. Panels (.cz-panel) are reserved for textured/patterned backgrounds only — plain bands don't need them. Snow text reads directly on colour at display size.

markup
<section class="cz-band cz-band--teal">
  <div class="cz-container">
    <span class="cz-eyebrow">How to celebrate</span>
    <h2>Nine essentials</h2>
    <p>Body copy.</p>
  </div>
</section>
.cz-band--cream · dusk ink · dusk corner-02

Snow — the default ground

Dusk ink on snow: 5.69:1 — passes AA for body copy. This is the base of the system. Corner-02 botanical ornaments always appear on snow bands, coloured to match the headline ink. 180px minimum — never smaller on a full-width band.

Warm accent ground — eyebrow and heading moments. Snow copy sits directly on flame without a panel for display-scale text. For body copy at small sizes, add a panel. No illustration here — the flame does the work.

Snow circle, sunset-dominant icon — the icon's primary fill matches the band colour so it reads as embedded in the world, not dropped on top. Snow text on sunset is legible at display size without a panel.

12 — Decisions you might want to change

Flagging what I chose rather than you

  1. Muted text is dusk at 72% alpha (--cz-ink-soft), not an eighth colour. Keeps the palette at seven; costs a little control. Over bread or flame it drops to about 4:1.
  2. Errors are sunset, success is dusk. No red exists. On dark bands, error flips to flame for contrast.
  3. Sunset and valentine are accent-only grounds. Enforced by convention and the panel pattern, not by code.
  4. Germania One stops at h3. Pushing it to h4 means legibility complaints at 19px.
  5. The header emblem forces a tall header at its 160px floor. The alternative is a small wordmark for the header, which doesn't exist yet.
  6. No shadows at all. A lifted card would be a deliberate exception to argue for.
